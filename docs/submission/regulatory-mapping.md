# Corridor Wealth — Regulatory design mapping, not certification

This is a clause-level engineering comparison for the synthetic Topic B prototype. It is not a legal opinion, regulatory approval, suitability certification or permission to offer investment services. The correspondence below is our interpretation of the cited official texts. A passing software test establishes only the stated technical behavior.

This F5 edition maps the integrated implementation. BIAC allocation, the M4 output guard, M5 suitability/retake controls and the M1 approval budget are implemented and included in the [generated rule/test inventory](rule-matrix.md). Runtime outcomes are sourced separately from [FINAL-RESULTS](../../results/FINAL-RESULTS.md). Successful engineering tests do not establish legal compliance.

## Scope and source selection

A commercial bank and a wealth-management subsidiary have different institutional roles. This mapping does not claim an actual issuer, distributor, licence, institutional affiliation or endorsement. All prototype institutions and products are fictional.

For a bank issuing its own non-principal-guaranteed wealth product, the 2018 bank wealth measures are the relevant starting point. For a bank distributing a wealth-management company's product, the 2021 sales measures and the 2025 bank agency-sales measures address distributor responsibilities. The issuer and distributor remain separate regulated parties. Plain deposits must not be silently treated as non-principal-guaranteed wealth products. Actual product classification, permissions and distribution contracts remain **to be verified** before any real deployment. The scope provisions are included in the tables below.

Official-source verification date: **2026-10-17 UTC**. The Chinese texts were checked against official regulator or government reproductions; final enacted texts were used, not consultation drafts. The NFRA suitability measures apply from **1 February 2026** (Article 49). A further disclosure regulation applies from **1 September 2026** and is included because it is already relevant to the competition period. EU and US provisions are comparative references only; they do not confer a Chinese banking licence or determine the obligations of a Chinese bank.
